# Example: Adoption KPI Tree for an AI-supported drafting workflow

**Status:** Filled example, fictional
**Template:** [`adoption-kpi-tree-template.md`](../frameworks/adoption-kpi-tree/templates/adoption-kpi-tree-template.md)

The organisation, team and numbers below are invented. The example shows how the template reads once it is filled in, not the results of a real rollout.

**Fictional setting:** Harrow & Lane, a publishing company with about 600 employees. Its explainer desk (14 editors, 2 designers, 1 desk lead) writes around 40 recurring explainer articles a month. The desk is piloting an AI-supported drafting workflow: a writing assistant with approved sources, a briefing template and a fixed review step.

## 1. Context

### Use case or workflow

```text
AI-supported first drafts for recurring explainer articles (rules, deadlines, how-to topics).
```

### Scope

```text
Included: briefing, first draft, fact check against approved sources, editor review.
Not included: opinion pieces, interviews, breaking news, anything with personal data.
```

### Target group or roles

```text
Editors (14), desk lead (1), designers (2) for the visual summary, legal contact on request.
```

### Adoption maturity stage

```text
[ ] Exploration
[x] Pilot
[ ] Early rollout
[ ] Stabilization
[ ] Scaling
[ ] Operating model integration
```

## 2. Strategic adoption ambition

```text
Enable the explainer desk to use AI-supported drafting for recurring explainers so that editors spend
less time on first drafts and more on checking and sharpening, while every published claim stays
traceable to an approved source and an editor signs off each article.
```

## 3. Adoption outcomes

```text
1. Recurring explainers start from the briefing template, not from a blank page.
2. Editors know which article types are in scope and which are not.
3. Every AI-supported draft passes the same fact check and editor sign-off.
4. Review time goes into substance (accuracy, audience fit), not into rewriting style.
5. Prompts and briefings that work are shared and reused across the desk.
```

## 4. Behavioral and workflow signals

```text
1. Share of recurring explainers that start with the briefing template.
2. Editors who completed at least three AI-supported drafts on their own.
3. Drafts that reach review with all source links filled in.
4. Fewer requests to the desk lead asking "may I use it for this?"
5. New briefing patterns added to the shared library each month.
```

## 5. Signal categories

```text
[x] Usage signals
[x] Activation signals
[x] Workflow signals
[ ] Capability signals
[x] Quality signals
[x] Value signals
[x] Risk signals
[x] Trust signals
[ ] Governance signals
[ ] Scaling signals
```

### Selected signal categories

```text
Usage and activation show whether editors try the workflow at all.
Workflow and quality show whether it fits real work and holds the editorial standard.
Value shows whether the time saving is real.
Risk and trust matter because explainers carry rules and deadlines that readers act on.
Scaling stays out until the pilot has three stable months.
```

## 6. Quality indicators

```text
1. Corrections requested at editor review per draft (target: falling over the pilot).
2. Published corrections after release (target: no increase against the last 6 months).
3. Drafts with every claim linked to an approved source.
```

## 7. Value indicators

```text
1. Time from briefing to reviewed draft (baseline: measured manually for 4 weeks before the pilot).
2. Editor hours per explainer, split into drafting and checking.
3. Reuse rate of shared briefings.
```

## 8. Risk indicators

```text
1. Drafts reaching review without source links.
2. Use outside scope (opinion, interviews, personal data) spotted in review.
3. Editors accepting AI wording without changes on factual passages.
```

## 9. Countermetrics

| Metric | Countermetric | Why it matters |
| ------ | ------------- | -------------- |
| More AI-supported drafts | Corrections at review | Volume is worthless if review becomes rewriting. |
| Shorter time to reviewed draft | Published corrections | Speed must not move errors to the reader. |
| Higher reuse of shared briefings | Reader feedback on sameness | Reuse should not make every explainer sound alike. |

## 10. Operating actions

```text
If drafts reach review without source links in more than 1 of 10 cases,
then the briefing template is not doing its job.
Action:
Make the source field mandatory and rerun a 20-minute session for the desk.
```

```text
If time to reviewed draft falls but corrections at review rise,
then editors are fixing AI wording instead of checking substance.
Action:
Review three drafts together in the desk meeting and adjust the drafting instructions.
```

```text
If questions about scope stay frequent after week four,
then the scope rules are unclear.
Action:
Publish a one-page "in scope / out of scope" list with examples next to the template.
```

## 11. Decision ownership

| Decision area | Owner | Decision forum | Possible action |
| ------------- | ----- | -------------- | --------------- |
| Workflow fit | Desk lead | Weekly desk meeting | Change template, change scope |
| Quality | Managing editor | Monthly quality review | Pause article types, tighten review |
| Risk/governance | Managing editor with legal contact | Monthly quality review, ad hoc on incidents | Stop use for an article type |
| Enablement | Desk lead | Weekly desk meeting | Sessions, pairing, shared briefings |
| Scaling | Editor-in-chief | Quarterly portfolio review | Extend to a second desk |

## 12. Review rhythm

```text
Owner: Desk lead
Review frequency: weekly during the pilot (12 weeks), then monthly
Decision forum: weekly desk meeting; monthly quality review for quality and risk
Escalation path: desk lead → managing editor → editor-in-chief
Revision trigger: any published correction caused by an AI-supported draft
```

## 13. Interpretation notes

```text
More scope questions in weeks one to three are a good sign: editors are testing the boundary instead of guessing.
High usage only counts as adoption if corrections at review and after publication stay stable.
Low usage from two or three editors usually means the article types they write are out of scope, not that they refuse the workflow. Check before acting.
```
